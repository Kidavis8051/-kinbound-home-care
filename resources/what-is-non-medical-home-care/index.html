Understanding your options

What does non-medical home care include?

"Non-medical" tells you what it isn't. Here's what it actually is.

Non-medical home care covers the everyday support that helps someone stay safely and comfortably at home, without involving clinical treatment. It's a broad category, so it helps to break it down into what it actually looks like day to day.

Personal care

Hands-on help with the physical routines of the day: bathing, dressing, grooming, toileting and routine mobility assistance. This is the category most people picture first when they think of home care.

Companion care

Conversation, social engagement, supervision and a familiar presence. This category exists specifically to address isolation and provide a safe set of eyes on someone's day, even when hands-on personal care isn't the main need yet.

Homemaker services

Meal preparation, light housekeeping, laundry and general household upkeep — the tasks that keep a home itself safe and functional.

Dementia and memory support

Routine, structure, cueing and patient redirection for someone living with memory loss, along with the personal care and safety supervision that usually go alongside it.

Respite care

Scheduled relief for family caregivers, covering the same non-medical needs above so a spouse or adult child can step away, rest, or handle other responsibilities.

What non-medical home care does not include

It's just as important to know the boundary. Non-medical home care does not include skilled nursing, medication administration or injections, wound care, two-person or mechanical-lift transfers, or medical transportation. Those fall under home health or skilled nursing, not home care.
