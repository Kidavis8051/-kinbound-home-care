Hospital discharge

What happens after a hospital discharge when Mom still needs help?

Discharge paperwork covers the medical plan. It rarely covers who's going to help her get dressed next Tuesday.

Hospital discharge planning is thorough about medical follow-up — medications, appointments, home health orders if needed. What it often doesn't fully address is the everyday reality waiting at home: who helps her bathe, who's there if she's unsteady on her feet, who makes sure she's eating.

The gap between "medically ready" and "ready at home"

A patient can be medically cleared to leave the hospital while still being far from independent. Recovering from a fall, surgery or illness often means temporary or ongoing help is needed with the basic routines of the day — exactly the kind of support that falls outside home health's scope.

Questions worth asking before discharge

  • Can she safely get in and out of bed, and to the bathroom, on her own?
  • Is someone available to help with bathing and dressing during recovery?
  • Who's preparing meals and managing the household while she regains strength?
  • Is there a safety concern about being alone for long stretches during the day?

If the honest answer to any of these is "we're not sure," it's worth arranging non-medical support before discharge day arrives, rather than scrambling once she's already home.

How non-medical home care fits alongside medical follow-up

Home care can run in parallel with any home health or outpatient therapy your loved one is receiving. While a nurse or therapist addresses the clinical side of recovery, a caregiver can help with personal care, meal preparation and household tasks, and simply being present so she isn't recovering alone.

If you're a discharge planner or case manager

We work directly with hospital case managers, social workers and rehabilitation professionals across the Omaha area. Visit our healthcare professionals page to learn about our referral process.

Getting started quickly

Discharge often comes with short notice. If you're trying to arrange support before someone comes home, reach out to our care team as soon as you have a sense of timing — the sooner we understand the situation, the sooner we can have a caregiver in place.
